## What and why

`GetOrderbookHeatmap` now returns its historical chain as a replay of the
top-N view that the request's `depth` selects. Before this change, page
limits could reconstruct different books for the same timestamp. The base
keyframe was cut to the requested depth, but the deltas carried changes at
every depth. When a delta deleted a level inside the top N, the level that
moved up into view had never been sent.

- `chain.base_keyframe` holds the top-N levels per side of the requested
  depth. Levels with a zero quantity are never listed.
- Each `chain.deltas[]` bucket lists the changes to that view:
  - A level that enters the top N appears with its quantity, even when the
    bucket did not change it, for example after a better level was deleted.
  - A level that leaves the top N appears with quantity `0`, even when it
    still rests deeper in the book.
  - Changes deeper than the top N are omitted.
- Applying the deltas over the base, oldest first, gives exactly the top-N
  levels per side after every bucket, the same at every page `limit`:
  - `CLOSE`: the order book at the bucket's close. It equals the
    `base_keyframe` of a chain anchored at that bucket.
  - `PEAK`: each price's highest quantity during the bucket, among the
    prices that held liquidity during it. A price that closed a bucket empty
    leaves the view in the next bucket in which it holds no liquidity.
- The base keyframe is the book from strictly before the first returned
  bucket. In `PEAK` mode it is only the replay's starting point, not a bucket
  view: it holds the peaks of the bucket it was advanced to, or a stored
  keyframe's recorded peaks, and can differ between page limits. Do not render
  it as a bucket. Only the replay after each bucket is the same at every page
  limit.
- Replayable history starts after the oldest stored keyframe: the bucket at
  that keyframe is no longer listed.

- A finalized live bucket (`HeatmapLiveBucket` with `is_final: true`) can be
  published again when later updates for it arrive. The new payload holds
  every update of the bucket and a higher `book_seq_end`. Keep the payload
  with the highest `book_seq_end` per `ts_sec`. Finalized buckets are
  delivered at least once: the same finalized payload can arrive more than
  once, for example after a service restart; keep the highest
  `book_seq_end` per `ts_sec`. Updates of a bucket still open are best
  effort; a later update or the final payload supersedes them. A market's
  buckets are published live only once the service projects the market
  live: a bucket that holds only earlier updates is history, available
  through `GetOrderbookHeatmap` and never published live. The newest
  buckets in
  `GetOrderbookHeatmap` follow the same rule: a later read can return a
  bucket with more updates and a higher `book_seq_end`.

- `updates_in_bucket` no longer counts the feed's 30-second refresh snapshot
  when it repeats the book unchanged: such a refresh is not an update.

The message shapes, fields and pagination tokens are unchanged.

## How clients use it

Request a chain as before, then replay it without clipping:

```bash
curl -sS https://<api-host>/marketdata.v1.HeatmapService/GetOrderbookHeatmap \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"symbolId": 1, "interval": "INTERVAL_1M", "depth": "DEPTH_50", "quantityMode": "CLOSE", "limit": 60, "timeRange": {"endTime": "2026-11-18T12:00:00Z"}}'
curl -sS 'https://<api-host>/v1/spot/markets/BTC-USDT/heatmap?interval=1m&depth=50&quantityMode=CLOSE&limit=60&endTime=2026-11-18T12:00:00Z'
```

For each side, start from the base levels, then apply each bucket oldest
first: set the quantity of every listed price, and delete the price when the
quantity is `0`. The book after a bucket has at most `depth` levels per side,
and every page that contains the bucket gives the same book after it. In
`CLOSE` mode it also equals the base keyframe of a page anchored at that
bucket.

## Migration

None for clients that replay the chain as documented: they get the same top
N, now consistent at every page limit. Clients that kept the base and applied
deltas without removing levels pushed out of view now receive those evictions
as quantity `0`. Clients that read levels deeper than the requested depth from
the deltas must request a deeper `depth` instead. In `PEAK` mode, a price no
longer lingers at an old peak after it emptied: it leaves the view once a
bucket passes without liquidity at that price. The oldest bucket of the
retained history, the one at the oldest keyframe, is no longer returned.
Clients of the live stream that keep the first final payload of a bucket
must keep the one with the highest `book_seq_end` instead.
