## What and why

No field, message or behaviour changes. The documentation of
`orders.v1.OrdersService.CancelAllOrders` (REST `POST /v1/orders/cancel-all`)
now states which orders a cancel-all covers and how it is ordered with the
account's other requests. Automated emergency stops rely on this:

- Unless `dry_run` is set, what a cancel-all cancels is fixed when its
  `request_id` is first admitted: every matching order of the account
  admitted before that, from any interface, and none admitted after it.
- An order whose create, batch create, modify or batch replace response
  arrived before the cancel-all was sent was admitted before it and is
  covered. This includes an order not yet reported open and an order that
  replaced an earlier one.
- Requests still in flight when the cancel-all is sent have no defined order
  with it, and neither do requests whose outcome is unknown. An order they
  create may be admitted after the cancel-all and stay open.
- The response confirms admission, not completion. Each covered order
  eventually ends; any still open when the cancellation applies is canceled.
- A retry with the same `request_id` returns the original outcome and covers
  no order admitted after the original request. A rejected cancel-all, or one
  whose outcome is unknown, is no barrier.

## How clients use it

To stop trading, first let the account's outstanding order requests answer,
then send the cancel-all with a fresh `request_id`:

```text
await every in-flight CreateOrder / BatchCreateOrders / ModifyOrder / BatchReplaceOrders

POST /orders.v1.OrdersService/CancelAllOrders
{"requestId": "stop-2026-11-17T08:30:00Z"}

POST /v1/orders/cancel-all
{"requestId": "stop-2026-11-17T08:30:00Z"}
```

If you cannot wait for every in-flight request, send the cancel-all at once
and send another, with a new `request_id`, after the last of them has
answered. Follow order updates, or read open orders, for each order's final
state. Triggers are not canceled and can place orders after the cancel-all;
cancel them separately.

## Migration

None: behaviour is unchanged. A client that sends a cancel-all while order
requests are still in flight, and expects it to cover them, must wait for
their responses first or send a second cancel-all once they have answered.
