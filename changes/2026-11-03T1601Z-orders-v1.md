## What and why

This note states the precision of `orders.v1.Order.cum_notional` and of the
quote-used figures derived from it, which the change that added the order
execution totals (`cum_notional` and `fee_total`) left out. Each fill
settles its quote rounded down to 18 decimal places. So `cum_notional` (REST
`cumQuote`) is exactly the quote the fills settled when the pair's
`base_quantity_scale` is at most 9. Above that, it can exceed the settled
quote by less than 1e-18 per fill. No field, enum value or status changes.

## How clients use it

The fields and the calls that read them are unchanged
(`OrdersReadService.GetOrder`, `GetOrderHistory`, `GetOpenOrders`; REST
`GET /v1/spot/orders/<order-id>`, `GET /v1/orders/history` and
`GET /v1/orders/open`):

```bash
curl -sS https://<api-host>/orders.v1.OrdersReadService/GetOrder \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' -H 'Authorization: Bearer <token>' \
  -d '{"orderId": "<order-id>"}'
```

The quote a buy sized by a quote amount used, and the amount it left unspent,
computed from `cumQuote` and `feeTotal` as before, are exact when the pair's
`base_quantity_scale` is at most 9. Above that, the used figure can exceed the
settled debit by less than 1e-18 per fill. Read `base_quantity_scale` from the
pair's spot configuration.

## Migration

None: nothing on the wire changes. A client that compares `cumQuote` with
settled balances at full 18-decimal precision should allow a difference below
1e-18 per fill on pairs whose `base_quantity_scale` is above 9.
