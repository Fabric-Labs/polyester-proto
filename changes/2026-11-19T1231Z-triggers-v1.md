## What and why

The documentation now says exactly what a cancel of one take-profit or
stop-loss ends. Behavior does not change. Before, the `CancelTrigger` text
said that cancelling either leg of an OCO cancels both, which could be read as
covering the take-profit and stop leg attached to an order with `oco` set.

- A leg of an OCO created with `CreateOco`, a trigger with an
  `order_list_id`: cancelling either leg with `CancelTrigger` cancels both,
  as `CancelOco` does.
- An attached take-profit or stop leg of an order, a trigger with a
  `parent_order_id`: `CancelTrigger` ends only that leg, whether or not the
  order's `RiskPolicy.oco` is set. The other leg stays active and keeps
  protecting the position.
- `RiskPolicy.oco` decides only what canceling a leg's working exit order
  ends: with `oco` set, both legs end; without it, only that leg ends.

## How clients use it

To drop the take-profit of an order and keep its stop-loss, cancel the
take-profit trigger:

```http
POST /triggers.v1.TriggersService/CancelTrigger
{"triggerId": "<take-profit trigger ID>"}

DELETE /v1/triggers/<take-profit trigger ID>
```

The stop-loss stays active. To end both attached legs, cancel each leg, or,
with `oco` set, cancel the working exit order of either leg.

## Migration

None: behavior is unchanged. A client that expected `CancelTrigger` on one
attached leg of an `oco` order to end the other leg too should cancel the
other leg as well.
