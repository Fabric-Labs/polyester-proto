## What and why

Markets can now carry a price band, which limits how far from the market an
order may be priced or trade, as on other major venues.

- `marketdata.v1.PairConfig.price_band` (`PriceBand`, field 25) publishes a
  pair's band in `GetSpotConfig`: `lower_bps` and `upper_bps`, the deviations
  below and above the band's reference price in basis points, and
  `reference_window_ms`, how long the reference stays fixed before it can move
  to a newer trade. The reference follows the pair's last trade and moves to
  the pair's index price when the two diverge by more than the band. The
  field is absent for a pair without a band.
- A buy limit order priced above the upper limit, or a sell limit order priced
  below the lower limit, is rejected with the existing
  `ERROR_CODE_PRICE_BAND_VIOLATION` (60). A replace that would add exposure
  beyond the band is rejected the same way; reducing an order's quantity is
  always allowed.
- A market order, or another order whose price the exchange sets (such as a
  market stop's or a capped trailing stop's order), trades only up to the
  band's limit. Its unfilled remainder is canceled with the new
  `orders.v1.CancelReason` value `CANCEL_REASON_PRICE_BAND` (20). The band
  never cancels a resting order, and a limit order priced away from the market
  on the passive side (a buy far below, a sell far above) is accepted.
- An attached take-profit, stop-loss or trailing stop whose order the band
  refuses stays active and is sent again once the band moves; a standalone
  trigger whose order the band refuses fails with
  `TRIGGER_FAILURE_REASON_PRICE_BAND_VIOLATION` (14).

## How clients use it

Read the band with the pair's other constraints:

```http
POST /marketdata.v1.MarketDataService/GetSpotConfig
{}
```

```json
{"pairs": [{"symbol": "BTC-USDT", "tickSize": "0.01",
  "priceBand": {"lowerBps": 500, "upperBps": 500, "referenceWindowMs": 60000}}]}
```

With reference price R and tick size T, the band's limits are
`ceil(R × (1 − lowerBps / 10000) / T) × T` and
`floor(R × (1 + upperBps / 10000) / T) × T`. Clients do not see R itself;
the last trade price is a close estimate, so validating a limit order against
`last × (1 ± bps / 10000)` before sending it avoids most rejects.

Order reads (`OrdersReadService.GetOrder`, `ListOrders` and the order events)
report `cancelReason: CANCEL_REASON_PRICE_BAND` on a market order the band
stopped; its fills before the cancel stand.

## Migration

None: the field and the enum value are new. A client that maps cancel reasons
exhaustively should add `CANCEL_REASON_PRICE_BAND`; until it does, the value
decodes as an unknown enum value.
