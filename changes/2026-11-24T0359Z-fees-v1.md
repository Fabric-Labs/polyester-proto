## What and why

`GetSpotFeeRates`' `reserve_fee_rate_percent` documentation now describes a
fee cap fixed when an order is accepted. An order that can only take is a
market order, or an IOC or FOK limit order such as a buy sized by a quote
amount. Its fees are capped at the lower of `reserve_fee_rate_percent` and the
account's `taker_fee_rate_percent` in force when the order is accepted, and a
buy paying its fee in the quote asset prices its fee reserve at that cap.
Before, only a buy sized by a quote amount and paying its fee in the quote
asset had this cap. A standalone stop-loss, take-profit or
trailing stop whose order can only take fixes the cap when the stop is
created, and each slice of a TWAP whose slices can only take when that slice
is placed. Take-profit and stop-loss orders attached to an order or paired in
an OCO, and orders that can rest, stay capped at `reserve_fee_rate_percent`
(a post-only one at `post_only_reserve_fee_rate_percent`).

Each fill is still charged the rate of the VIP tier and fee policy current
when the fill is settled, now never above the order's cap, whatever its side
and fee asset. The cap is an upper bound on every fill, not a fixed rate: a VIP
tier change after the order is accepted can move a later fill's rate up or
down within it, for example 0.01 % after an upgrade and back to 0.03 % after a
downgrade, but never above the cap. No field, message or RPC changes.

## How clients use it

Nothing changes in the calls. A client can estimate the cap of an order it is
about to place from the market's row:

```text
POST /fees.v1.FeeService/GetSpotFeeRates
{"symbolId": [1]}
```

The estimate is `min(reserveFeeRatePercent, takerFeeRatePercent)` of the row.
It is not a guarantee: a VIP tier or fee policy change before the order is
accepted changes the cap the order gets. Once the order is accepted, its cap
is fixed. Fees are charged in the quote asset on each fill's notional, or for
a buy paying its fee in the base asset, in base on the quantity it receives.

A fixed-quantity buy that can only take and pays its fee in the quote asset
reserves its fee at the cap, so when the cap is below `reserveFeeRatePercent`
it needs less available balance than before. A buy sized by a quote amount
still holds its whole specified budget; a lower cap lets that budget buy more.

## Migration

None: the wire and the field values are unchanged. A client that sized a
taking buy's balance from `reserveFeeRatePercent` keeps working; it may now
size it from the lower rate.
