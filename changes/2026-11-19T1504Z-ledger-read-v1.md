## What and why

The private balance stream now carries `ledger.read.v1.BalanceUpdate`, so a
client can keep its Trading balances exact without polling: within an epoch
every Trading change arrives in order and numbered, so a missed update shows as
a gap, and a repeat after a reconnect is dropped by its position.

- A TRADING update is one step of the account's Trading balances. It carries
  its position, `(trading_epoch, trading_sequence)`, whether the Trading values
  are a consistent snapshot after it (`complete`), the assets it changed with
  their Trading fields only, the Trading transfer legs it applied, and
  `trading_applied_through_ts_ns`: every Trading transfer at or before that
  time is in these balances.
- A FUNDING update carries one asset's Funding balance and its
  `funding_revision`, with no Trading fields.

Each publication used to be one `AssetBalance` with both the Trading and the
Funding fields of one asset, and a Trading change could be superseded by a
later one before delivery.

`AssetBalance` drops `trading_revision`: the position replaces it.

`GetBalances` now returns the position its Trading values are at:
`trading_epoch`, `trading_sequence` and `trading_applied_through_ts_ns`. Its
Trading values are one atomic cut: every asset is taken at that single
position. Its answer reflects every balance update published before the call
began (across an ownership move, its earlier Trading transfers: see "How
clients use it"); when it cannot yet, it answers `503` / `unavailable`, which can be
retried with a short backoff. A call costs 1 unit of the `trading_read`
rate-limit class, whatever the number of assets.

`trading_epoch` is a plain unsigned 64-bit number that changes when the
numbering restarts. Compare it only for equality: an epoch you dropped
means drop the update, and any other epoch than the current one means
reload.

`TransferRow` gains `ts_ns`, the exact transfer timestamp in nanoseconds, in
`ListTransfers`, on the transfer stream and on balance updates.

## How clients use it

Subscribe to `private:ledger:balances:{accountPublicId}` (JSON) or
`private:ledger:balances:{accountPublicId}:proto` (protobuf
`ledger.read.v1.BalanceUpdate`), then:

1. Buffer the stream's updates.
2. Call `GetBalances` and apply its balances. Remember its position (E, Q):
   `tradingEpoch` and `tradingSequence`. If it answers `503`, retry it with
   a backoff.
3. Drop buffered and later TRADING updates of epoch E with a sequence at or
   below Q. Apply E's sequences Q + 1, Q + 2, and so on, in order.
4. Keep a dropped set of epochs: every epoch you held before E, and every
   epoch other than E of an update received before the `GetBalances` call
   that answered E began. `GetBalances` never answers a dropped epoch again,
   so drop a TRADING update of a dropped epoch without reloading: after a
   move, the earlier epoch's late updates can still arrive. Reload, from
   step 2, when:
   - a TRADING update's epoch is neither E nor dropped, including one
     buffered while the call ran: the numbering restarted;
   - its sequence skips a number: an update was missed;
   - after an incomplete update of E (`complete` false), E's next sequence
     does not arrive within 2 seconds. FUNDING updates, repeats and updates
     of dropped epochs do not stop the timer;
   - the subscription reports that missed publications could not be
     recovered.

   So a move usually costs one reload, and at most one more if the earlier epoch's
   late update is the first you see of that epoch.
5. Apply a FUNDING update when its `fundingRevision` is higher than the
   asset's.

Tell the two kinds apart by `account`, never by the epoch: a FUNDING update
has no `tradingEpoch` or `tradingSequence` (unset, zero in protobuf), and a
TRADING update's epoch is never zero.

`GetBalances` reflects every balance update published before the call began,
so a reload covers anything the subscription missed in the answer's epoch, even
a final update of an idle account, and an earlier epoch's Trading transfers as
below. No heartbeat is needed.

Show Trading values after an update with `complete` set: in between, the same
step continues in the next update. The updates of a new epoch up to its first
`complete` one carry every asset the account holds in Trading.

After a move, a new epoch's Trading values can be older than the previous
epoch's last ones: its owner may not have applied the latest transfers yet.
Until its `tradingAppliedThroughTsNs` reaches the last one you saw, the epoch
is catching up, and its later updates apply those transfers. Their legs can
then arrive a second time, in the new epoch: `(transferId, isDebit)`
identifies a leg. `GetBalances` waits, or answers `503`, rather than answer
such a cut below a Trading transfer the stream published in an earlier epoch,
including one you never received. Right after a server restart it may not know
every such transfer, so still count a transfer as applied only by a receipt in
the current epoch: its leg, or a `tradingAppliedThroughTsNs` at or past its
`tsNs`.

```bash
curl -sS 'https://<api-host>/v1/balances' \
  -H 'Authorization: Bearer <session token>'
# ConnectRPC: POST https://<api-host>/ledger.read.v1.LedgerReadService/GetBalances {}
```

The REST answer (ConnectRPC returns the same fields in protobuf JSON):

```json
{"balances": [{"symbol": "USDT", "trading": "250.25", "reserved": "25.125",
  "available": "225.125", "funding": "1000.5", "fundingRevision": "81"}],
 "tradingEpoch": "4294967303", "tradingSequence": "42",
 "tradingAppliedThroughTsNs": "1767225600123456789"}
```

A TRADING update on the JSON channel:

```json
{"account": "trading", "tradingEpoch": "4294967303",
 "tradingSequence": "43", "complete": true,
 "tradingAppliedThroughTsNs": "1767225600123456789",
 "balances": [{"symbol": "USDT", "trading": "250.25", "reserved": "25.125",
   "available": "225.125"}],
 "transfers": [{"transferId": "340282366920938463463374607431768211455",
   "amount": "25.125", "symbol": "USDT", "transferCode": "trade_quote",
   "account": "trading", "timestamp": "1767225600123456",
   "tsNs": "1767225600123456789", "balanceAfter": "250.25", "isDebit": true}]}
```

A FUNDING update on the JSON channel:

```json
{"account": "funding",
 "balances": [{"symbol": "USDT", "funding": "1000.5", "fundingRevision": "81"}]}
```

On the balance channel and in REST, `tradingEpoch`, `tradingSequence`,
`tradingAppliedThroughTsNs`, `tsNs` and a leg's microsecond `timestamp` are
decimal strings of unsigned 64-bit values. On the transfer channel,
`timestamp` stays a JSON number and `tsNs` is a string. An update carries at
most 64 assets and 50 transfer legs.

To tell whether a transfer from `ListTransfers` or the transfer stream is
already in the Trading balances, compare its `tsNs` with the
`tradingAppliedThroughTsNs` of an update or a `GetBalances` answer of the
account's current epoch: the watermark is a receipt within its epoch.

## Migration

1. Decode the balance stream as `BalanceUpdate` (protobuf) or the new JSON
   object, instead of one asset's balances per publication.
2. Read `account`: take only the Trading fields from a TRADING update and only
   the Funding fields from a FUNDING update, and keep the other fields from
   your earlier state.
3. Stop reading `tradingRevision`: order Trading values by the position and
   reload on an epoch that is neither the current one nor dropped, a gap,
   an incomplete update whose next sequence does not arrive within 2 seconds
   or a failed recovery, and drop the late updates of a dropped epoch, as in "How clients use it".

`GetBalances`, `ListTransfers` and the transfer stream otherwise only gain
fields: clients that ignore them need no change there.
