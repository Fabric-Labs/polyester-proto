## What and why

Statement exports limited to one asset now identify every entry exactly as
exports of every asset do. An entry is one ledger operation, such as a trade
with its fees. Its `entry_id`, `time`, `type`, `link_id` and `flow_id` come
from all of the operation's movements on the account, in every asset, so
the same trade carries the same `entry_id` in an all-assets export and in an
export limited to its quote asset. `entry_id` is the `transfer_id` of the
entry's first transfer leg on the account within the export's period.

The contract text of `entry_id` now states its scope. It is the same
whichever asset the export is limited to, over the same period. A period
boundary can fall between the legs of one entry; each export then holds its
own part of the entry under the `transfer_id` of that part's first leg.

The contract text of `STATEMENT_EXPORT_FAILURE_REASON_TOO_LARGE` now states
the limit's scope. The limit is 4,000,000 ledger movements of the account in
the period. Every asset's movements count, even for an export limited to one
asset, because each entry is built from all of its movements before the
asset's rows are selected.

No message, field or enum value changes. Only documentation text changes.

## How clients use it

Create and read exports as before. To reconcile exports of the same period
made with different asset filters, join rows on `entry_id` together with
`account` and `asset`: for the same period, the `entry_id` is the same
whichever asset filter produced the file.

```http
POST /v1/statements/exports
{"idempotencyKey": "usdt-2026-11", "startTime": "2026-11-01T00:00:00Z", "endTime": "2026-12-01T00:00:00Z", "assetId": 7}
```

The ConnectRPC equivalent is
`/ledger.read.v1.StatementExportService/CreateStatementExport` with the same
JSON body.

An export limited to one asset fails with
`STATEMENT_EXPORT_FAILURE_REASON_TOO_LARGE` when the account's movements in
the period, across all assets, exceed 4,000,000. Split the period into
shorter exports.

## Migration

Exports of every asset are unaffected.

An export limited to one asset is now refused with
`STATEMENT_EXPORT_FAILURE_REASON_TOO_LARGE` when the account has more than
4,000,000 movements in the period across all assets, even if the selected
asset has far fewer. Such a period could succeed before. Clients that export
one asset over long periods for very active accounts must:

1. Treat `STATEMENT_EXPORT_FAILURE_REASON_TOO_LARGE` as a possible outcome
   of an asset-filtered export, and do not retry the same period.
2. Split the period into shorter exports, for example one per month or per
   week, until each holds at most 4,000,000 of the account's movements.
3. Expect a split to divide an entry. A period boundary can fall between
   the movements of one entry, such as a trade's two legs. Each file then
   holds its own part of the entry, and the later file shows its part
   under a different `entry_id`, the first of that part's movements.
   `entry_id` only matches across asset filters over the same period, so
   reconcile across split periods by amount and balance as well.

Files limited to one asset that were produced before this change may show a
different `entry_id` for the same trade than an all-assets file. Create a
new export to get the stable identifiers.
