## What and why

The documentation of an iceberg order's display quantity now states how
modifying the order treats it. No field, message or method changes.

- Lowering an iceberg order's quantity in place to its display quantity or
  below is allowed. The order keeps its queue priority and then shows all of
  its remaining quantity: it reads back with `display_qty_scaled` unchanged
  and `hidden_qty_scaled` zero.
- A modify that replaces the order (a new price with behavior `AMEND_OR_REPLACE` or
  `REPLACE_ONLY`, or any `REPLACE_ONLY` change) keeps the display quantity
  while the new quantity is above it. When the new quantity is at or below
  the display quantity, the new order is a plain, fully visible limit order
  with `display_qty_scaled` zero, and it stays plain if its quantity is
  raised later.
- `AMEND_ONLY` still refuses a price change with
  `ERROR_CODE_MODIFICATION_REQUIRES_REPLACE`.
- Placing an iceberg order still requires a display quantity below the
  order's quantity (`ERROR_CODE_ICEBERG_DISPLAY_INVALID`).

Before this change, an iceberg lowered to its display quantity or below
could no longer change its price: every replace was refused with
`ERROR_CODE_ICEBERG_DISPLAY_INVALID`.

## How clients use it

Lower the quantity, then change the price:

```json
POST /orders.v1.OrdersService/ModifyOrder
{"orderId": "8432716352103424", "symbolId": 1, "requestId": "shrink-1", "newQtyScaled": "10000", "behavior": "AMEND_OR_REPLACE"}

POST /orders.v1.OrdersService/ModifyOrder
{"orderId": "8432716352103424", "symbolId": 1, "requestId": "reprice-1", "newPriceTicks": "65184910000000", "behavior": "AMEND_OR_REPLACE"}
```

If the first call leaves the quantity at or below the display quantity, the
second answers with a new order ID, and `GetOrder` on it reports
`displayQtyScaled` and `hiddenQtyScaled` zero.

## Migration

None: no client code changes. A client that shrinks an iceberg and then
reprices it should expect a fully visible order. To rest a hidden quantity
again, cancel it and place a new iceberg order.
