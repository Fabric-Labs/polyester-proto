## What and why

An order's attached take-profit, stop-loss and trailing-stop legs now protect
every fill of the order, including fills that settle after one of its legs
fired. Before, a market order that filled at several prices could cross its
own take-profit with its first fill: the take-profit fired at once, and both
legs, and the take-profit's exit order, kept the quantity of that first fill
only, so the later fills had no take-profit. Now:

- Each live leg's `qty_scaled` follows the order's whole settled position,
  less what its exit orders sold, also while one of its exit orders works.
- When the take-profit's exit order ends, a fill that settled after the
  take-profit fired is sold by a new exit order of that leg, while the last
  trade still meets its trigger price. Otherwise the leg waits for the next
  trade through its trigger.
- The stop-loss keeps protecting what the take-profit's exit has not sold.

No message, field or enum changes.

## How clients use it

Read a leg's quantity as before: `GetTrigger` and `ListTriggers`
(`/triggers.v1.TriggersService/GetTrigger`) return each attached leg with its
`parent_order_id`, and its `qty_scaled` is now the position it protects. An
attached leg can show a larger quantity than its working exit order, whose
`qty_scaled` is what that order sells, and the leg can place one more exit
order (its next child sequence) for fills that settled after it fired.

```http
POST /triggers.v1.TriggersService/GetTrigger
{"triggerId": "<take-profit leg ID>"}
```

The leg's `qtyScaled` in the answer is the order's settled position less
what its exit orders sold, for example the whole 0.0566 of a market buy
that filled at two prices, while its first exit order still works for the
0.0465566 of the first fill.

## Migration

None: the wire is unchanged. A client that sized its display of a working
leg from its first exit order should read the leg's own `qty_scaled`.
