## What and why

`trading_applied_through_ts_ns`, on `GetBalancesResponse` and on each TRADING
`BalanceUpdate` of the balance stream, is a receipt: every Trading transfer of
the account at or below it is applied in those balances. Its contract now says
what happens when a transfer of the account could not be applied. The value
stays below that transfer until a later balance of the same asset includes it,
so it never passes a transfer that is not applied. Before this change, such a
transfer could fall at or below the value while the balance did not include
it. No field, message or RPC changes.

## How clients use it

Unchanged: compare a transfer's `ts_ns` (from the transfer stream or
`ListTransfers`) with `trading_applied_through_ts_ns` of a `GetBalances`
answer or a TRADING update in the same `trading_epoch`:

```
POST /ledger.read.v1.LedgerReadService/GetBalances
{}
```

When `ts_ns` is at or below `trading_applied_through_ts_ns`, the transfer is
applied in those balances. While a transfer of the account was not applied,
the value can stay where it is even as other accounts' balances move on; it
moves past the transfer once a later balance of that asset includes it. Until
then it also stays below the account's later transfers, of any asset, even
those already applied: for those, the transfer's leg on the balance stream is
the receipt, and the balances themselves are current.

## Migration

None. A client that waits for the receipt to reach a transfer keeps working;
it never gets a receipt for a balance that does not include the transfer, and
while one of the account's transfers is not applied it can wait longer, past
transfers that are, until a later balance includes the missing one. Keep a
timeout on such waits, as before, and take the transfer's leg as its receipt
when the stream delivered it.
