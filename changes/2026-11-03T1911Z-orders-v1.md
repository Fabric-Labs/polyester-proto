## What and why

The contract now states how exact an order's `cum_notional` is. Each fill
settles its quote rounded down to 18 decimal places. `cum_notional`, the sum
over the order's fills of their quantity times their price, in quote units
scaled by 10^(9 + `base_quantity_scale`), is therefore exactly the quote the
fills settled when the pair's `base_quantity_scale` is at most 9. Above that,
it can exceed the settled quote by less than 1e-18 per fill. Behavior is
unchanged; it was not documented, and a client reconciling a buy's quote use
against its balance could not tell whether the two must match exactly.

## How clients use it

Read the order as before and compute what a buy sized by a quote amount used:

```bash
# Authenticate as for any private read (omitted here).
curl -sS https://<api-host>/orders.v1.OrdersReadService/GetOrder \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' -d '{"order_id": "1234567890123456789"}'
# "cumNotional": {...}, "feeTotal": {"amountE18": {...}, "isRebate": false, "qtyScaled": "..."}
curl -sS 'https://<api-host>/v1/spot/orders/<base58-order-id>'
# REST carries the decimal "cumQuote"
```

- used = `cumQuote`, plus the fee total when the fee is paid in quote, or
  minus it when it is a rebate;
- unspent = `submittedMaxQuoteDebit` − used.

These figures are exact when the pair's `base_quantity_scale` is at most 9.
Above that, the used figure can exceed the settled debit by less than 1e-18
per fill.

## Migration

None: no message, field or enum value changes. A client that compares
`cum_notional` with the settled balance change should allow the documented
difference on pairs whose `base_quantity_scale` exceeds 9.
