## What and why

Each firing of a bot's schedule calls the bot, in each of a backtest's three
cost scenarios, when its code handles the schedule. These calls now count
toward the window an unset `StartBacktestRequest.start_time` resolves to,
together with candle and `on_start` calls. Before, that window was sized from
candle and `on_start` calls alone, so a schedule's firings could take a
backtest without a start past its budget of 30,000 calls, and it failed: a bot
on a 15-minute schedule alone, 96 firings a day, could get a year of them. Now
that bot gets at most its most recent 104 days, and a bot on a 1-minute
schedule alone at most 6. The calls combine: a bot keeps 365 days only when
its candle, timer and `on_start` calls together over a year fit. An hourly
schedule alone keeps 365 days, and so does an hourly candle series alone, but
a bot with both makes 48 calls a day and gets 208.

A finished minute bot's report warning now names candle and timer calls as
the ones a default window is sized to. Calls from orders and price levels
still aren't counted ahead. Only comments and that warning text change; no
field or value changes.

## How clients use it

```http
POST /strategies.v1.StrategiesService/StartBacktest
{"versionId": "sha256:...", "idempotencyKey": "..."}
```

Read the window the backtest resolved to from the backtest:

```http
POST /strategies.v1.StrategiesService/GetBacktest
{"backtestId": "..."}
```

`backtest.window` holds its start and end. A request that gives
`startTime` keeps it.

## Migration

None for a request with `startTime`. Without one, a bot's default window,
and so its backtest identity, changes only when adding its schedule's
firings moves the window its candle and `on_start` calls alone gave. A
1-minute series with a 15-minute schedule and `on_start` stays at 6 days,
with the same identity: its firings fit those 6 days. With a year of data, a
15-minute schedule alone moves from 365 days to 104, and an hourly series
with an hourly schedule from 365 to 208. Each then gets a new identity, and
its earlier default backtests held more calls than the budget, so they
failed. A retry with the same idempotency key still returns the backtest it
first started.
