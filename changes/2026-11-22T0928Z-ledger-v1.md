## What and why

Dust conversions now appear in an account's transfer history. Before, a conversion changed the account's balances but left no transfer rows, so the running `balance_after_e18` of later rows jumped by the payout with nothing to explain it.

`ledger.v1.TransferCode` gains `DUST_CONVERT` (1077; REST `dust_convert`). One conversion writes:

- one debit row per converted source asset, for the whole amount converted;
- one credit row in the target asset, for the payout after the fee.

The fee is taken from the payout. It is not a separate transfer, so the rows add up exactly to the balance change of every asset the conversion touched. The gross amount and the fee stay on the conversion itself (`transfer.v1.DustConversionService`, `GetDustConversion` and `ListDustConversions`). The other side of every dust conversion row is a `PRIVATE_COUNTERPARTY`.

The same rows arrive on the account's realtime transfer stream and as legs of Trading balance updates.

## How clients use it

`ListTransfers` returns the rows with the other transfers, and can filter on them:

```http
GET /v1/transfers?transferCode=dust_convert&reversed=true
```

The ConnectRPC path is `/ledger.read.v1.LedgerReadService/ListTransfers` with the body `{"transferCode": "DUST_CONVERT", "reversed": true}`.

A conversion's rows do not necessarily appear together. One source's debit can reach the history and the transfer stream before the payout's credit does, and rows of other transfers can come between them. Treat each row on its own. Rows carry no conversion ID (`link_id` is 0), so to show a conversion as a whole, with its gross amount and fee, read it from `ListDustConversions` or `GetDustConversion`.

## Migration

None. The enum value is new. A client that renders unknown transfer codes generically keeps working, but should give `DUST_CONVERT` its own label, for example "Dust conversion".
