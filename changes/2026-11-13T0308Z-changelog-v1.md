## What and why

`ListPosts` and `ListChanges` now order posts published at the same time by
release, as the changelog lists them everywhere else. Several releases deployed
together go live at the same instant, so their release posts share one
`published_at`. Ordering those by slug as text put `v0.92.9` above
`v0.92.10`.

Posts are ordered by `published_at` descending. At the same `published_at`,
release posts (posts with `release_version`) come first, by release version
descending, comparing major, minor and patch as numbers. The other posts follow
by slug descending, as before. No message, field or RPC changes.

## How clients use it

Nothing new to call. A page of posts at one instant now reads, for example:
`v0.92.10`, `v0.92.9`, `v0.92.8`, then the other posts of that instant by slug
descending.

```bash
curl -sS https://<api-host>/changelog.v1.ChangelogService/ListPosts \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' -d '{"limit": 20}'
curl -sS 'https://<api-host>/v1/changelog/posts?limit=20'
```

`ListChanges` lists the changes of those posts in the same post order, or its
reverse with `SORT_ASC`. Changes keep their publication order inside each post.

Paging is unchanged: pass `next_page_token` back as `page_token`. Tokens stay
opaque. Paging through posts that share an instant neither skips nor repeats
a post, also when a post is withdrawn between pages.

## Migration

None. Page tokens issued before this change remain accepted. Clients that
sort posts themselves should use the order above instead of comparing slugs as
text.
