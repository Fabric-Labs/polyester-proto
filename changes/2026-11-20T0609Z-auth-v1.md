## What and why

Connected apps can now ask for a second OAuth scope, `strategies:manage`,
together with `account:read`. It lets the app build, test and propose
trading bots. It can't deploy a bot or move funds: the user deploys a
proposed bot on the website. The consent text also names stopping running
bots, which the scope will cover once stopping a bot is part of the API.

`AppConnectionRequest.scopes` and `ConnectedApp.scopes` can now hold
`strategies:manage` next to `account:read`. No message, field or RPC
changed; only the documented scope values.

With `account:read`, a connected app also reads the account's bots:
strategy explanations, backtests, deploy proposal status, bots and bot logs.
With `strategies:manage`, it also validates strategies, runs their scenarios,
starts backtests and proposes deploys.

## How clients use it

The consent page reads the requested scopes as before and shows one entry
per scope:

```text
POST /auth.v1.ConnectedAppService/GetAppConnectionRequest
{"requestId": "<request id from the consent URL>"}

-> {"connectionRequest": {"client": {...}, "scopes": ["account:read", "strategies:manage"], ...}}
```

Show `strategies:manage` as: "Build, test and propose trading bots, and stop
running bots. It can't deploy a bot or move funds."

An app asks for it in its authorization request, always together with
`account:read`, for example `scope=account:read strategies:manage`. A request
for `strategies:manage` alone is refused with `invalid_scope`.

## Migration

None: existing grants keep `account:read` only, and a client that shows the
scope strings it receives keeps working. A consent page or connected-apps list
that maps scope strings to labels should add a label for `strategies:manage`.
